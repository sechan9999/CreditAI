READING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  best on the population you can see    hard cutoff
  best on the population you cannot     none (accepts only)

  These disagree, which is the whole argument. Selecting a
  reject-inference method on the accepted population picks the wrong one.

  Lift over doing no reject inference at all, on the declined population:
    hard cutoff            AUC -0.0001   KS +0.0002
    fuzzy augmentation     AUC -0.0010   KS -0.0012
    parceling              AUC -0.0011   KS -0.0073

  The last two columns are the business question: if this model were used to
  reconsider the declined applicants, how many would it approve, and what
  share of those were actually good? The declined base rate is 39.7%.

  CAVEAT: true_good exists here because the data is synthetic. A real lender
  has no such column, which is why reject inference exists. What this shows
  is which method to reach for -- not a measurement you can repeat in
  production without deliberately approving a randomised slice of declines.
