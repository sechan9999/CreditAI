CALIBRATION -- the part that matters for a scorecard.
The deployed model uses class_weight="balanced", which reweights the classes
and therefore calibrates probabilities to a 50/50 prior rather than to the
population base rate. The score maps probability to points as if the
probability were the real one, so a shift here shifts every score.
  Logistic (balanced) - deployed   A: accepts only    mean p 0.479 vs actual 0.379  Brier 0.2103
  Logistic (balanced) - deployed   B: + parceling     mean p 0.578 vs actual 0.379  Brier 0.2420
  Logistic (unweighted)            A: accepts only    mean p 0.379 vs actual 0.379  Brier 0.1994
  Logistic (unweighted)            B: + parceling     mean p 0.384 vs actual 0.379  Brier 0.2010

Decomposed, the balancing is almost entirely an intercept shift:
  slope correlation between the two fits   0.999979
  largest slope difference                 0.0045
  intercept difference                     +0.4923 in log-odds

A constant log-odds shift times the scale factor is a constant point shift:
  0.4923 x 57.7078 = +28.4 points on every applicant

So the weighting buys no ranking -- a monotone shift cannot change AUC, and it
does not: both fits score 0.7252. What it changes is the meaning of the number.
The policy says approve at p(good) >= 83.3%. If the probabilities are inflated,
the applicants being approved do not meet that bar, so the system is not
implementing its own stated policy. Fixing the calibration does not change the
policy; it makes the implementation match it.

Two ways to fix it: drop class_weight="balanced", which costs nothing in AUC;
or keep it for fitting and calibrate the probabilities on a holdout (Platt or
isotonic) before the points mapping. Either changes approval volume, so it is
a decision to make deliberately rather than a silent correction.
